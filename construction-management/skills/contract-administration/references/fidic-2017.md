## 2. Claims procedure (20.2): applies to both Employer and Contractor claims

1. **20.2.1 Notice of Claim:** as soon as practicable and no later than **28 days** after the claiming Party became aware, or should have become aware, of the event. If it is late, the claiming Party is **not entitled** to relief. The Engineer must give Notice within **14 days** if it considers the notice late; if it doesn't, the Notice of Claim is deemed valid, although the other Party may still dispute this.
2. **20.2.2 Engineer's initial response:** the 14-day time-bar notice described above.
3. **20.2.3 Contemporaneous records:** keep them. The Engineer may inspect them without admitting liability.
4. **20.2.4 Fully detailed Claim:** within **84 days** of awareness. It must include the contractual and/or legal basis; if that basis isn't submitted within the period, the Notice of Claim lapses, subject to the Engineer's Notice within 14 days.
5. **20.2.5 Agreement or determination:** under 3.7. The Engineer may take into account whether the late submission prejudiced the other Party and whether the other Party had prior knowledge, and on that basis may accept a late submission as valid.
6. **20.2.6 Continuing effect:** interim fully detailed Claims monthly; the final Claim within 28 days of the end of the effects.

## 3. Agreement or determination (3.7)

- 3.7.1 Consultation to encourage agreement. If there is no agreement within **42 days**, the Engineer gives Notice and moves to determination.
- 3.7.2 A fair determination, in accordance with the Contract.
- 3.7.3 Time limit: **42 days** after the start date. For a claim, the start date is receipt of the fully detailed Claim (for a matter, the relevant start). If the Engineer gives no Notice of agreement or determination in time, the claim is **deemed rejected** (or the matter is deemed a Dispute).
- 3.7.4 Effect: binding, subject to correction within 14 days.
- 3.7.5 **Notice of Dissatisfaction (NOD) within 28 days** of the Notice of the determination; otherwise the determination becomes **final and binding**.

## 4. Disputes (Clause 21)

- 21.1 A standing DAAB is appointed at the start of the contract; 21.3 covers avoidance of disputes (informal assistance).
- 21.4.1 Refer the Dispute to the DAAB within **42 days** of giving or receiving the NOD under 3.7.5, otherwise the NOD lapses.
- 21.4.3 DAAB decision within **84 days** of the referral (or as agreed).
- 21.4.4 NOD against the DAAB decision within **28 days**; otherwise it becomes final and binding.
- 21.5 Amicable settlement: arbitration may start on or after the **28th day** after the NOD.
- 21.6 Arbitration (ICC unless stated otherwise).
- 21.7 Failure to comply with a DAAB decision can be referred directly to arbitration.

## 5. Notable changes from 1999

- Claims procedure applies symmetrically (Employer claims go through 20.2 as well).
- 84-day fully detailed claim period (was 42 days), with explicit deeming provisions.
- The Engineer must act neutrally in 3.7 (not "on behalf of the Employer").
- Advance warning (8.4) and more detailed programme requirements (8.3).
- Standing DAAB, with a dispute-avoidance role.
- "Exceptional Events" (Clause 18) replaces Force Majeure.
- Increased use of deemed provisions: silence can create an outcome.
