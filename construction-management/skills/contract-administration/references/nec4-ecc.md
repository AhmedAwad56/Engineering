# NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract: Key Points

The periods below are for the unamended form. The Contract Data (for example the "period for reply") and Z clauses may change them.

## 1. Main Options

| Option | Pricing | Contractor's risk |
|---|---|---|
| A | Priced contract with activity schedule | Highest (lump sum by activity) |
| B | Priced contract with bill of quantities | Quantities risk with the Client |
| C | Target contract with activity schedule | Pain/gain share |
| D | Target contract with bill of quantities | Pain/gain share |
| E | Cost reimbursable | Lowest |
| F | Management contract | — |

Common secondary Options: X2 (changes in the law), X5 (sectional completion), X7 (delay damages), X16 (retention), W1/W2 (adjudication; W2 is for UK Construction Act contracts).

## 3. Early warnings (15)

Both the Contractor and the PM must give an early warning as soon as they become aware of a matter that could increase the Prices, delay Completion or a Key Date, or impair performance. If the Contractor fails to give an early warning that an experienced contractor could have given, the CE is assessed as if the warning had been given (63.7), which may reduce the CE's value.

## 4. Programme (31–32)

The Accepted Programme is central to NEC4. CEs are assessed against it, so an unaccepted programme weakens the Contractor's position, and the PM can assess a CE itself if there is no Accepted Programme. Clause 31.2 lists the required contents: dates, float, time risk allowances, the order and timing of work, Client work, access dates, Key Dates, planned Completion, and the health and safety requirements.

Clause 50.5: until the first programme is submitted, one quarter of the Price for Work Done to Date is retained.

## 5. Compensation events (60–66)

1. Notification: by the PM (61.1, for its own instructions) or by the Contractor (61.3).
2. The PM decides whether it is a CE and instructs a quotation (61.4).
3. The quotation shows the changes to the Prices (Defined Cost + Fee, forecast) and any delay to planned Completion, using a revised programme (62.2).
4. The PM accepts the quotation, instructs a revised quotation, or makes its own assessment (62.3, 64).
5. Once implemented (65), the CE isn't revisited, even if the forecast proves wrong.

The main CE triggers are listed in 60.1: (1) change to the Scope, (2) access, (3) Client not providing something, (4) instruction to stop work, (5) Client/others not working within times, (6) late reply, (12) physical conditions, (13) weather (1-in-10-year test), (14) Client's liabilities, (18) Client's breach, (19) prevention events, and others.

## 6. Payment

For Options A and B, see 50–51. For Options C, D and E, the amount due is Defined Cost + Fee, per the Schedule of Cost Components. The PM may disallow cost under 11.2(26) (Disallowed Cost).

## 7. Disputes

- W1 (non-Construction Act): the dispute is notified and referred to the Adjudicator within the periods stated in the W1 table. These depend on the type of dispute and are typically between 2 and 4 weeks, so check the table.
- W2 (UK Construction Act): either Party may refer to adjudication at any time.
- W3: a Dispute Avoidance Board.
- After adjudication: notify dissatisfaction within the stated period (typically 4 weeks), then go to the tribunal.
